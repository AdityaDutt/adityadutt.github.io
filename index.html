I develop machine learning for scientific data where sensors are heterogeneous and labels are scarce: aligning modalities in shared latent spaces, generating realistic synthetic data to train downstream models, and structuring embeddings so they transfer across domains. Applications include remote sensing, plant root phenotyping from X-ray CT, crop phenotyping from drone imagery, computational pathology, and socioeconomic mapping.

I am a Postdoctoral Associate in the Machine Learning and Sensing Lab at the University of Florida, working with Dr. Alina Zare. I received my Ph.D. in Computer and Information Science and Engineering from UF in 2025.

On the job market: tenure-track faculty positions, 2026–27.

Research

COMMANet shared manifold
(JSTARS 2022)

Multimodal alignment and fusion

Mapping hyperspectral, LiDAR, SAR and optical data into one class-discriminative space, so one model serves every sensor and a missing sensor can be predicted from the others.

JSTARS 2022 · IGARSS 2025
Root generation figure
(CANVAS 2026)

Generative modeling for scientific data

Translating simulated measurements into realistic ones: sampled root skeletons condition generated 3D root geometry, which is then rendered as synthetic X-ray CT of soil cores, so segmentation models can be trained without costly manual annotation.

Dissertation 2025 · CANVAS 2025 · CANVAS 2026
JUGAAD indicator map
(arXiv 2026)

Structured latent spaces under limited supervision

Contrastive and triplet objectives that shape embedding geometry, autoencoder representations for downscaling survey indicators across India, and weakly supervised graph networks trained on synthetic and incomplete labels.
